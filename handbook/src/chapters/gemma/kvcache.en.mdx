# KV cache: generating token by token

<p className="lead">A model writes its answer one token at a time, and every new token needs a full forward pass. Without care, each pass recomputes the image, the prompt and the whole answer so far, again and again. The KV cache keeps exactly what does not change, so each step only has to process the one new token.</p>

## What changes, and what does not?

Think about one layer when token 263 is being generated. To compute its output the layer needs:

- **Q of the new token**: it is the one asking the question.
- **K and V of every token**: the past ones and the new one.

The Q's of the past tokens are not needed any more: their outputs were produced in earlier steps. And the K and V of a past token **never change**. With the prefix-LM mask, the prefix only looks at the prefix, and every answer token only looks backwards (<Link to="/bridge">3</Link>). So nothing that comes later can reach back and change an earlier token's K or V, in any layer. Recomputing them is pure waste.

That is the whole idea: store K and V per layer, and from then on feed the model one token per step. It is called a KV cache, not a QKV cache, because Q is never stored.

## Concept questions

<ConceptQ n={1} q="Why does the prefill need a mask, while a single generation step does not?">
The prefill processes many tokens at once, so it has to block what each of them may not see: the prefix-LM mask keeps the image and prompt bidirectional and the rest causal. A generation step has one token, the last one. Everything before it is allowed, and nothing after it exists yet, so the mask row is all zeros.
</ConceptQ>
<ConceptQ n={2} q="How much memory does the cache take for PaliGemma at 224 px?">
Per token: layers × 2 (K and V) × KV heads × head_dim × bytes = 18 × 2 × 1 × 256 × 2 = <b>18 KB</b> in bf16. With about 270 tokens (260 prefix + a short answer) that is <b>~5 MB</b>. With 8 KV heads instead of 1 (plain multi-head attention) it would be 8× more. At 448 px the image alone is 1024 tokens, so ~4× more.
</ConceptQ>
<ConceptQ n={3} q="Why is the cached output not bit-identical to the uncached one in bf16?">
The math is the same, but the matmuls have different shapes (1 row instead of T rows), so the floating-point sums can run in a different order. In float32 the difference is ~1e-6. Greedy decoding still picks the same tokens unless two candidates are almost tied.
</ConceptQ>
